What the measures mean

Read task-set quality as several complementary measures.

Read the measures together

A useful comparison needs tasks that separate models, cover a range of difficulty, and produce interpretable outcomes. No single measure establishes all three. Information and reliability and task diversity add further context.

Discrimination

Discrimination is the fraction of tasks that some models pass and others fail. A task passed by every model, or by none, does not distinguish the evaluated models. A high value means the set separates this group of models; it is not a model score.

Difficulty coverage

Coverage describes how widely task success rates span easy to hard. It is high when tasks occupy several difficulty bands and low when most tasks fall into one band. Difficulty here is measured from the evaluated models, not from an author’s label.

Consistency of model ordering

This measure asks how often a model with a clearly higher overall success rate also succeeds on tasks passed by a lower-scoring model. Reversals can motivate closer inspection, but may reflect different strengths rather than a faulty task.

The measure can be high simply because the models are too similar to separate. Read it alongside discrimination and uncertainty.
