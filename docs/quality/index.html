the small-N caveat

readiness advisory

The "measured" column is read from real gate-scored matrices of the current day. A handful of tasks is far too few to certify these numbers. Read the column as the current reading of a thin day, not as a property of the benchmark.

how to read these numbers

A ranking is not a result.
At a handful of tasks per model, one model’s Wilson interval covers most of the others.
D is coarse at small N.
It takes a few discrete values; one task flipping moves it a large step.
Not yet one instrument.
Below the conventional KR-20 bar the items are not demonstrably one ability, so the total is not a skill estimate.
A vacuous high is not a win.
M reaches 1.0 when no ability separation is left to violate — absence of evidence against the set, not evidence for it.
Required-N is an order of magnitude.
It assumes the next tasks are drawn like today’s, and its bootstrap SE is itself noisy.
Readiness is advisory.
It never gates an accept, never touches the reward path, and cannot promote or demote a task.

What it is for: catching a collapsed set — every task all-pass or all-fail, an item correlating negatively with total score, a difficulty band missing.

It builds the (task × model) matrix, excluding records with model == "oracle". It prints the axes and the readiness line, then emits the card as JSON. It requires at least 1 task and 2 models, and exits 2 otherwise.
